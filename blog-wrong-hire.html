I hired him. Months later, I was part of the decision to let him go.

Nov 3, 2026TalentLeadership

He was a salesperson I genuinely believed in. He went through our full process, met the right people, and gave us every reason to think he'd succeed. Over time, his numbers told a different story.

It was the right business decision. It was also a hard one. I had believed in him, and I wanted to make sure he left feeling respected.

But once it was over, I kept going back to the hiring decision.

Did I miss a signal? Ask the wrong questions? Trust my own read too much? Or was the issue somewhere else: a gap between the role we hired for and what success in that role eventually demanded?

Those are two very different problems. One is an assessment failure. The other is a role-definition failure.

They need very different fixes.

What I took from it:

→ A bad outcome doesn't automatically mean a bad decision. But it's never a reason to stop examining one.

→ Agreeing on measurable 6-month success criteria with the hiring manager before the first interview.

→ Revisiting those criteria at 90 days, once the role has had time to shift.

When a hire doesn't work out, where do you look first: the person, the assessment, or the environment we expected them to succeed in?
