We started with the employee's performance. We ended with the manager's.

Oct 28, 2026LeadershipHRBP

A manager came to me frustrated with an employee who wasn't showing enough initiative or making the progress expected of them. The gap was affecting the team, and the manager knew something had to change. But when it came to addressing it, he hesitated. He wanted to be fair, protect the relationship, and avoid discouraging the employee.

As we unpacked the situation, it became clear that the challenge wasn't just how to handle the employee's performance. It was how to hold someone accountable without feeling that the relationship would suffer.

I've seen this tension play out in different ways. Managers postpone the conversation, soften the message, or hope things will improve on their own. Not because they don't care, but because the immediate discomfort feels greater than the cost of waiting.

But waiting has a cost, too.

The employee doesn't get a real opportunity to understand the gap, the manager grows more frustrated, and the team can end up carrying the consequences.

Leadership means accepting that a difficult conversation may strain a relationship in the short term, while recognizing that avoiding it can do greater damage over time.

In your eyes: what makes these conversations hardest for you, and what has helped you get past that hesitation?
